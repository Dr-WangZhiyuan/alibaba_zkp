The Strange Cave of Ali Baba

An interactive journey through Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP), told through the tale of a magical cave

The Story

1

The Strange Cave

In Baghdad, old Ali Baba chased thieves into a cave that forked into two passages, left and right. Each passage ended in a dead end, yet every thief who fled inside vanished without a trace. After forty thieves escaped this way, Ali Baba knew it could not be mere luck.

The Setup: A secret exists
2

The Secret

Hiding inside the cave, Ali Baba discovered the truth: the magic words "Open Sesame" slid open a hidden wall connecting the two dead ends. Anyone who knew the words could enter one side and exit the other. Ali Baba changed the magic words and caught the next thief.

The Secret = A private key or password
3

Mick Ali's Challenge

Centuries later, Ali Baba's descendant Mick Ali wanted to prove to a television reporter that he knew the cave's secret, but without revealing the magic words themselves. His goal: convince the world he possesses the knowledge, while keeping the secret safe.

The ZKP Challenge: Prover (P) proves without revealing
4

The Demonstration

Mick enters the cave alone and picks a passage at random. The reporter, unable to see which way Mick went, walks to the fork and calls out a side: "Come out on the left!" or "Come out on the right!" If Mick knows the secret, he can always obey by opening the wall when needed. They repeat this forty times.

Interactive Proof: P (Mick) convinces V (Reporter)
5

The Jealous Reporter

A rival reporter filmed a fake version using a look-alike who did not know the secret. By editing out the failed attempts, the fake tape looked identical to the real one. Since no one could tell the tapes apart, the real tape proved Mick's knowledge without transferring any information about the secret itself.

Simulation Argument = Zero-Knowledge Property

Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) Properties

A ZKP involves two parties: the Prover (P) and the Verifier (V). Both share a common input, the statement to be proven. The cave story illustrates the three properties of ZKP.

Completeness

In the story: Mick Ali (P) is honest and knows the magic words, so he can always open the wall and exit from whichever side the reporter (V) requests. He succeeds every time across all 40 rounds.

If P is honest, P will eventually convince V that the statement is true.

Soundness

In the story: An impostor (dishonest P) without the magic words has only a 50% chance each round. After 40 rounds, the odds of fooling V are about 1 in a trillion. The statement is false, and P cannot convince V. Try "Impostor" mode above!

P can only convince V if the statement is true.

Zero-Knowledge(ness)

In the story: V (the reporter) confirms that P (Mick) knows the magic words, but never learns what the magic words actually are. The verification record (tape) can also be simulated without knowing the password, as shown by the jealous reporter using a double.

V learns nothing about the statement aside from the fact that it is true.
